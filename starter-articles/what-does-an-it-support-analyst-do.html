An IT Support Analyst helps people use technology reliably. That might mean helping someone sign in to their account, diagnosing a slow computer or investigating why an office printer will not connect. It is often one of the most accessible starting points for an IT career because the work combines technical fundamentals with communication and structured problem-solving.

What does the job look like?

An analyst typically receives requests through a ticketing system, phone, email or chat. They ask questions to understand the problem, check for common causes, follow approved troubleshooting steps and document the outcome. Some issues can be resolved immediately. Others require escalation to networking, systems or security teams.

Five common helpdesk responsibilities

  • Resetting or troubleshooting account access after appropriate identity checks
  • Helping users with Windows, office applications and peripherals
  • Investigating Wi-Fi, VPN, DNS and other connectivity problems
  • Documenting support tickets, actions taken and next steps
  • Escalating incidents that exceed the team's permissions or expertise

Technical skills to learn first

Start with Windows navigation and troubleshooting, basic networking concepts such as IP addresses and DNS, account and password management, Microsoft 365 applications, and safe remote-support practices. Do not try to master every IT speciality before applying for your first role.

People skills matter too

A good analyst listens without making assumptions, explains actions in everyday language and keeps the user informed. Accurate ticket notes help colleagues continue an investigation when a case is escalated or passed to another shift.

An example of a first-line support ticket

A colleague says Outlook will not open. Rather than reinstalling Office immediately, you might ask whether the issue started today, check if other applications work, confirm available updates, inspect relevant error messages and follow your organisation's support procedure. The key skill is choosing the next sensible check, not memorising a single fix.

How to start learning

Practise documenting issues on your own device. Learn the difference between symptoms, causes and solutions. Write a short knowledge-base note after each exercise. These habits help you demonstrate practical thinking in an entry-level interview.
